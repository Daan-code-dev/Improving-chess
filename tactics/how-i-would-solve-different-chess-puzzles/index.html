Checks, Captures and attacks

400 Elo

This is a ~400 rated puzzle on chess.com. It's white's move. Try to solve it yourself first, and after that, you can check out the solution below.

My thought process

A lot of people might be tempted to take the black rook on f5 with the bishop. I mean, it's a free rook right? That is a mistake a lot of beginners make. Think about what I said earlier: checks, captured and attacks. This is the order you should use to solve every puzzle. I started with looking at checks. I quickly found Rxd8+. Then, I looked at what black can do. His only move is Rf8, after which I can just take on f8, and then it's mate. Taking on f5 doesn't even have to be considered when you know that Rxd8+ ends with checkmate. Bxf5 is a common mistake beginners make, because they see a free piece, and don't look further if there's anything better available.

Simplified Solution

1.Rxd8+ Rf8 2.Rxf8#. Bxf5 fails due to Nxd1

800 Elo

This is a ~800 rated puzzle on chess.com. It's white's move. Try to solve it for yourself first, and after that, you can check out the solution.

My thought process

Checks, captured and attacks. That's the first thing I looked at. White only has one check, Qf7+. Because white's rook defends the queen, black has to move the king to h8. Here comes the tricky part. After Kh8, I looked again for checks, captures and attacks, and I saw Qf8+. After black plays Rxf8, white can play Rxf8 and it's checkmate. A lot of players would play Rxc7 instead of Rf8, seeing that it attacks 2 rooks at the same time. However, Black can play Qe8, defending both of the rooks.

Simplified Solution

1.Qxf7+ Kh8 2.Qf8+ Rxf8 3.Rxf8#

1200 Elo

This is a ~1200 rated puzzle on chess.com. It's blacks move. Try to solve it for yourself first, and after that, you can check out the solution.

My thought process

I first looked at any available checks. I saw two, Rxg2+ and Rh1+. After Rxg2+, white takes the rook with it's king, and black has nothing. After Rh1+, black can play Rh8+, but white plays Kg1 and black didn't make any progress, except for losing his rook. Then I started looking at captures. I already dismissed Rxg2+, and Rxb6 does absolutely nothing, and white can choose which rook he takes. Then I looked for possible attacks, and this is a tricky one. I saw Rh8, threatening checkmate on h1. The only way white can prevent this is by moving his g pawn, moving his f pawn, playing Kf1. Take for example f3. I again started looking for checks. I immeadiately saw that Rh1+, even though it's not checkmate, still wins the rook on a1 after Kf2. Note that every other move to prevent checkmate is met with the same move. The hard part about this puzzle is finding Rh8, because it doesn't give a check or capture something.

Simplified Solution

1... Rh8 2.f3 Rh1+ 3.Kf2 Rxa1
